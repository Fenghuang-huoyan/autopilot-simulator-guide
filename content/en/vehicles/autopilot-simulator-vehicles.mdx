## 100+ vehicles, not just Tesla

The official game description states: "You can drive around on Autopilot or FSD with 100+ ICE,
Hybrid, and Electric vehicles." Despite the Tesla-inspired name and UI, the roster isn't
Tesla-exclusive — official promotional screenshots show a wide mix:

- **Tesla-styled models** — a Cybertruck-styled pickup and Model Y/Model 3-styled sedans appear
  repeatedly across official media. **Verified in gameplay**: a creator video's dashboard clearly
  labels one car **"Model 3 Long Range AWD"** — a specific trim name, not a guess.
- **Other electric and hybrid-styled vehicles** — additional EV-styled hatchbacks and crossovers
  appear in official "New Cars" promotional screenshots, distinct from the Tesla-styled models.
- **ICE-styled vehicles** — SUV and sedan body styles resembling mainstream gas-powered cars also
  appear in official screenshots (including a badge-style front end resembling a BMW-style grille).

In-game license plates follow an "APS-###" format, visible across official screenshots.

## How you actually get cars: an in-game economy, not just spawning

Two things not visible from official screenshots alone, confirmed by watching creator gameplay:

- There's a persistent **cash currency** displayed in the corner of the screen (a specific
  creator's series is literally built around growing it from 0 to 300,000+), plus dedicated
  **CARS**, **SHOP**, and **JOB** menu buttons always on screen.
- **Driving itself earns cash** — a creator's own on-screen caption states "driving is indeed a
  good way to get rich in APS." Separately, the **JOB** menu leads to at least one distinct
  minigame we saw directly (a pizza-topping assembly task with named ingredients like Pepperoni,
  Mushroom, and Olives) — confirming "jobs" are actual gameplay loops, not just a menu label.
- New vehicles arrive via an on-screen **"Vehicle Spawned Successfully"** notification after being
  acquired, rather than always being pre-parked in the world.
- The in-game phone's home screen shows app-style icons including one for the current car (labeled
  by name, e.g. "Model 3") and a separate **"Uber"**-labeled icon, suggesting a ride-hailing-style
  feature tied to vehicles exists — exact function `Unconfirmed`, not demonstrated on screen in the
  clip we watched.

## Fastest car: still not fully confirmed, but we have real speedometer readings now

No official top-speed list or stat sheet is published for this game, and no single video we
watched was a dedicated top-speed comparison across multiple cars — so naming one car as
"fastest" would still be a guess. What we can report instead, because we watched the footage
directly: a creator's in-game speedometer was observed reading **122 MPH** and, later in the same
session, **124 MPH**, while driving a car acquired through the cash economy described above. That
confirms speeds well above the in-city "Max 45" speed-limit signs exist at least on some road
type/vehicle combination — it does not tell us which specific car is fastest, or what the actual
top speed cap is.

## Unconfirmed for now

A full vehicle list with names, categories (ICE/hybrid/electric) and stats isn't published
anywhere official, and we haven't watched a video dedicated to comparing multiple cars
side-by-side. The exact cash cost of vehicles, and what the in-game "Uber" icon does, are also
`Unconfirmed`. This page will be updated once a verifiable source (an official vehicle list, or a
specific creator video with visible in-game evidence) is found.
